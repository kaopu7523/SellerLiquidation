## Amazon doesn't sell pallets to you directly

Amazon distributes liquidated inventory through authorized third-party liquidation companies rather than selling pallets to individual buyers itself. The main platforms buyers use are **B-Stock** (auction-based, described as Amazon's official liquidation partner), **BULQ**, **Liquidation.com**, **Direct Liquidation**, and **888 Lots** — each with a different mix of auction versus fixed-price listings, minimum order sizes, and shipping coverage.

## Three types of lots, three different risk levels

Terminology gets mixed together a lot in this space, but the distinctions matter:

- **Return pallets** — verified customer returns, usually sold with a manifest listing each item and its condition.
- **Amazon liquidation pallets** — unsold overstock, not returns.
- **Mystery pallets/boxes** — smaller, unmanifested lots sold on the appeal of "surprise" contents, and the riskiest of the three since nothing is verified in advance.

Condition grading (New / Like New / Used / Damaged) is the other major variable, and it drives resale value directly.

## What pallets actually cost

Reported pricing varies by category and source, but converges around a consistent range: single pallets commonly run from roughly **$300-$400** on the low end up to **$1,000-$4,000+** for mixed-category or electronics-heavy lots, and a full truckload (around 24 pallets) can start near **$8,450**. Across these platforms, liquidation companies are generally reported to be paying around **10-30 cents on the retail dollar** for the inventory before reselling it to buyers — so the manifest's listed "retail value" is not what you're paying, and it's not what the goods will realistically resell for either.

## The costs buyers commonly forget to count

Beyond the pallet price itself, a full cost picture includes freight (which can be substantial depending on distance from the warehouse), sales tax, time spent inspecting and testing items, repair or refurbishment costs, platform selling fees, and — outside FBA — per-unit shipping to the end customer. New buyers frequently underestimate this stack and judge a deal only on the headline pallet price.

## Red flags to watch for

Sources describing this market consistently flag a few warning signs: a full pallet priced under $100 (legitimate companies can't operate at that price), a seller who won't provide a manifest or any content detail, listing photos that show only premium electronics (a common bait-and-switch pattern), and a single SKU filling an entire pallet in a competitive category (which tends to mean everyone who buys that batch ends up competing to resell the same item). It's also worth knowing that liquidation companies sometimes "cherry-pick" the most valuable individual items out of a batch before packing and selling the remaining pallet — so a manifest's total retail value can look better than what's actually left inside.

Buyers are generally advised to budget for roughly **15-20% of a pallet's contents** being damaged, unsellable, or otherwise a write-off, even when buying from a reputable platform — and whether a resale license is required varies by platform rather than being a universal rule.
